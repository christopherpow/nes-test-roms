mset_delay.nes
Hyperkin Mouse read delay test, by Brad Smith 2019
http://rainwarrior.ca


Plug a Hyperkin mouse into the second controller port of your NES.
Use a regular controller in the first port.

Press Up/Down to adjust the read delay.
Press A to cycle sensitivity.
Press B to reset the current X/Y min/max.


This is a read speed test of the Hyperkin mouse.
Apparently it requires a small delay between the 2nd and 3rd bytes read.
It also has a speed limit on each bit read.

The delay between the two bytes (CPU cycle count) is adjustable by the controller.
Below 25 cycles the reported X/Y values were chaotic.
At 28 cycles or above they became completely stable.
25-27 have occasional glitches.

The bit read speed is fixed, but can be adjusted with MOUSE_BIT_DELAY in the source, and rebuilding.
An unrolled minimum of 11 cycles (MOUSE_BIT_DELAY=0) resulted in chaotic reported values.
At 14 cycles it became completely stable.
12-13 have occasional glitches.

The included ROMs are numbered by the cycles per bit read.


Otherwise the output of this test is very similar to the mset.nes mouse test ROM.


5 bytes are read each frame.
A record of the last 24 frames is displayed on the left hand side.

The top right displays the last report.
The first and last bytes there are raised to indicate they are less important.
(First byte should be 00, last byte is past the end of the report.)

Just beneath this are two numbers, the first is the read delay (cycles), and the second is the current sensitivity.


Below this are two numbers indicating the lowest and highest X displacement reported,
followed by two more numbers indicating lowest and highest Y displacement reported.
(Press B to reset the min/max.)

In the middle is the current offset displayed visually with a circle over a cross.


Source code requires CC65. (Place it in an adjacent cc65 folder, run build.bat.)
https://cc65.github.io/

build_runtime.bat is used to rebuild temp\runtime.lib (instructions are contained as comments)
preprocessor.bat is used to diagnose preprocessor usage
